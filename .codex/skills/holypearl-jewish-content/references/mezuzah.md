# Mezuzah editorial reference

## Core distinctions
The mitzvah concerns a correctly written parchment scroll containing Deuteronomy 6:4–9 and 11:13–21. A decorative case alone is not a mezuzah scroll. Catalog labels such as "נוסח אשכנזי" or "נוסח ספרדי" should be preserved as product titles, but explain scribal tradition separately; the Torah passages are not different prayer rites.

## Source anchors to inspect
- Deuteronomy 6:4–9 and 11:13–21: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.6.4-9 and https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.11.13-21
- Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 289:1: blessing and attachment.
- 289:2: right side on entering and beginning of upper third; special doorways need individual assessment.
- 289:6 with Rema: upright versus slanted placement. Many Sephardi/Edot HaMizrach communities follow upright placement; many Ashkenazi communities use a slant, top toward the room. Attribute the practice and leave room for family custom.
- https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Yoreh_De'ah.289
- https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/225414/jewish/Affixing-the-Mezuzah.htm : durable attachment, avoiding Shabbat/major festivals, blessing immediately before attachment where applicable. This is a Chabad practical source; do not universalize its list of doubtful rooms.

Do not hard-code shoulder height for all doors, say every opening requires a blessing, or claim a business is identical to a dwelling. Bathrooms, unusual entrances, uncertain direction, businesses, rentals and reattachment can need different treatment. Do not instruct readers to repair or mark parchment themselves.

A useful manual covers: identify obligated openings and resolve uncertainty; obtain a verified scroll and fitting protective case; establish side/height/custom; prepare durable attachment without damaging parchment; clarify whether the blessing applies; affix and check stability. A photo cannot establish the scroll's halachic validity.
